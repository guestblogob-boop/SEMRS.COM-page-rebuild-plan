A business line of credit gives you access to a set amount of money that you can draw, repay and draw again, paying interest only on what you use. That makes it a flexible way to fund marketing that changes month to month. This guide explains how it works, how it compares with a term loan, what lenders check and what it costs.

## Key takeaways

- A line of credit suits ongoing or uneven costs; a term loan suits one large project with a clear payback.
- Compare the full cost: rate, draw fees, annual fees and whether the rate is variable.
- Borrow for marketing only when you can track what it earns and repay from that income.

## How a business line of credit works

The lender approves a credit limit, say $50,000. You draw money when you need it, up to that limit. You pay interest only on the amount you have drawn. As you repay, the money becomes available again. Many lines have a draw period, after which you repay any balance, and the lender reviews the line each year.

Lines can be **unsecured** or **secured** by assets such as receivables or inventory. Secured lines are often larger and cheaper. Most small business lines also need a personal guarantee from the owner.

## Line of credit vs term loan

@img chart

| | Line of credit | Term loan |
|---|---|---|
| How you get money | Draw as needed, up to a limit | One lump sum |
| Interest | Only on what you have drawn | On the full amount from day one |
| Repayment | Flexible; reusable as you repay | Fixed payments over a set term |
| Rate | Often variable | Often fixed |
| Best for | Seasonal ads, stock, cash-flow gaps | A website build, a launch, equipment |

For marketing, the choice depends on the spending pattern. Monthly ad budgets that rise in busy seasons fit a line of credit. A one-off website project with a clear cost fits a term loan, because the fixed payments match a fixed project. ## Where to get a line of credit

- **Your bank or credit union:** often the lowest rates if you have a track record and good credit.
- **Online lenders:** faster decisions and looser requirements, usually at higher cost.
- **SBA-backed lines:** the SBA's 7(a) Working Capital Pilot offers monitored lines of credit of up to $5 million with terms of up to 60 months, for businesses with at least a year of operating history. They are offered through participating lenders.
- **Business credit cards:** a simple revolving line for smaller amounts. Our guide to the [best business credit cards](/best-business-credit-cards-for-advertising/) for ad spend compares the types.

## What lenders check

1. **Time in business**, often at least one or two years.
2. **Revenue and cash flow**, from bank statements and tax returns.
3. **Personal and business credit history.**
4. **Existing debt** and how much of your income it uses.
5. **Collateral**, for secured lines.
6. **Financial statements**, such as a profit and loss statement and balance sheet.

Prepare these before you apply. Clean, up-to-date books speed up the decision.

## Costs to compare

- **Interest rate:** fixed or variable, and what it is tied to.
- **Draw fees:** some lenders charge each time you draw.
- **Annual or maintenance fees.**
- **Origination fees** when the line opens.
- **Prepayment rules** and any penalty.
- **Renewal terms:** what happens at the yearly review.

Ask each lender for the total cost of a sample draw, such as $10,000 for six months. That makes offers easy to compare.

## Using credit for marketing wisely

Borrowed money should fund marketing you can measure. Before you draw:

- **Set up tracking** so you can see leads and sales from each channel. Our free [ROAS calculator](/tools/roas-calculator/) shows the return on ad spend.
- **Start with proven channels** and test new ones with small amounts.
- **Plan the repayment** from the revenue the campaign brings, not from hope.
- **Set a limit** for marketing draws so the line stays available for emergencies.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is a business line of credit?
It is a revolving credit limit that a business can draw from, repay and draw again. You pay interest only on the amount you use.

? Is a line of credit better than a loan for marketing?
A line of credit suits ongoing or seasonal marketing costs. A term loan suits a single large project with a known cost, such as a new website.

? How hard is it to get a line of credit for a business?
It depends on your time in business, revenue, credit and debt. Banks usually ask for more history; online lenders approve faster at higher cost.

? Does a line of credit need a personal guarantee?
Most small business lines do. That means you are personally responsible if the business cannot repay.
