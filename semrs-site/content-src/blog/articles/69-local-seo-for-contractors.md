Local SEO for contractors helps roofers, builders, landscapers, plumbers and other trades show up when nearby homeowners search for help. Most of these searches lead to a call within days, so being visible on the map and in local results brings real jobs. This guide covers the nine steps that matter most for a contracting business.

- Set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business with an honest service area.

## How contractors get found locally

Google shows local results in the map pack and in normal results. Its local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence. A contractor can't change distance, but can improve relevance with clear services and prominence with reviews, links and a strong website.

## Set your service area the right way

@img chart

Many contractors work from a home office and visit customers. Google calls these service-area businesses. Its guidelines say they should have one profile for the central office or location, with a designated service area.

- **Hide your home address** if customers don't visit you there, and show only the service area.
- **Keep it realistic.** Google says the overall service area shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from where you are based.
- **Use your real business name.** Google asks that the name match your real-world name, so don't add keywords or towns to it.

## Local SEO for contractors: the 9-step plan

1. **Complete your Google Business Profile.** Main category, extra services, hours, phone and booking link.
2. **Add job photos every month.** Before and after shots of real work, with the town in the caption.
3. **Ask every happy customer for a review.** Send a direct link the same day the job is finished.
4. **Reply to every review,** good or bad, politely and briefly.
5. **Build one page per service.** Roof repair, new roofs and gutters each deserve their own page.
6. **Add town pages for your main areas,** with real projects, local details and reviews from that town. Avoid copy-paste pages that only swap the town name.
7. **Keep your name, address and phone the same** on your website, directories and trade associations.
8. **Add local business schema** so search engines read your details correctly.
9. **Track calls and forms** to see which pages and searches bring jobs.

## What to put on a contractor's website

| Page | What it should include |
|---|---|
| Home page | Services, areas served, license, phone and reviews |
| Service pages | What the job involves, materials, timing and price guide |
| Town pages | Projects and reviews in that town, travel area |
| Projects or gallery | Real photos with short case notes |
| About | Years in business, team, licenses and insurance |
| Contact | Phone, form, service area and hours |

A local SEO company should be able to tell you which of these pages you are missing and why.

## Frequently asked questions

? How long does local SEO take for contractors?
Profile and review changes can help within weeks. New service and town pages usually take a few months to rank.

? Should I show my home address on Google?
Only if customers visit you there. Most contractors hide the address and show a service area instead.

? Do town pages still work?
Yes, when each one has real projects, local details and reviews. Thin pages that only swap the town name rarely help.

? Is local SEO worth it for small contractors?
Usually yes. Local searches come from people who need a job done soon, often in your area.
