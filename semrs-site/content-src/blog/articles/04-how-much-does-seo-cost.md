How much does SEO cost? Most businesses pay for SEO as a monthly retainer, and the most common price band is a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month. The right amount for you depends on how competitive your market is, how much work your website needs and whether you want local or national visibility. This guide explains the pricing models, shows typical ranges from published industry surveys and gives you a checklist for comparing quotes.

## How much does SEO cost? The short answer

In a widely cited Ahrefs survey of SEO providers, about 78% charged a monthly retainer. The single most common band was **$501 to $1,000 per month**, agencies averaged roughly **$3,209 per month** and freelancers roughly **$1,348 per month**. Providers in the US and Canada charged more than the global average. Treat these as reference points, not fixed prices: the survey was self-reported, and markets change.

| Pricing model | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly retainer | Ongoing work each month: technical fixes, content, links, reporting | Most small and mid-size businesses |
| Project fee | One-off audit, site migration or content project | A defined problem with a clear end |
| Hourly consulting | Advice and training billed by the hour | In-house teams that do the work themselves |
| Performance-based | Fees tied to rankings or leads | Rarely a good idea; incentives can favor shortcuts |

## What drives the cost of SEO

### Competition in your market

@img chart

Ranking a plumber in a small town is a different job from ranking a personal injury firm in a big city. The more businesses fight for the same searches, the more content, links and time it takes.

### The state of your website

A slow, poorly structured site needs technical work before content can perform. Migrations, broken redirects and duplicate pages all add hours.

### Local, national or international scope

Local SEO focuses on your Google Business Profile, reviews and city pages, so it usually costs less. National campaigns need more content and authority. Multi-language sites add translation and hreflang work.

### How much content you need

Content is often the biggest line item. A plan that adds four in-depth articles a month costs more than one that adds one, but it also builds authority faster.

### Who does the work

Freelancers usually cost less and suit smaller scopes. Agencies cost more but bring a team (technical, content, outreach, analytics) and continuity.

## What a fair SEO retainer should include

- **An audit and a written plan** for the first months, with priorities you can understand.
- **Technical fixes**: speed, indexing, structured data, broken links and mobile issues.
- **On-page work**: titles, descriptions, headings and internal links on the pages that matter.
- **Content**: new pages or articles each month, aimed at keywords with real demand.
- **Local SEO** if you serve an area: [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile-optimization/), reviews and citations.
- **Ethical link earning**: digital PR, partnerships, resources others want to cite.
- **Monthly reporting** from Google Search Console and Analytics, tied to leads or sales.

## Red flags in cheap SEO offers

- Guaranteed number-one rankings. Nobody controls Google's results.
- Hundreds of backlinks for a low fixed fee. Paid link networks break Google's spam policies.
- No access to your own accounts or data.
- Reports that show rankings for keywords nobody searches.
- Long contracts with no clear deliverables.

## How to set your SEO budget

1. **Start from the value of a customer.** If one new client is worth $3,000 over a year, a channel that brings two extra clients a month can justify a meaningful budget.
2. **Plan for six to twelve months.** SEO compounds; judging it after six weeks usually ends it before it works.
3. **Fund content and technical work first,** then links and expansion.
4. **Track leads, not just rankings.** Use call and form tracking so you can calculate cost per lead.

## SEO cost vs Google Ads cost

With Google Ads you pay for every click, and the 2025 WordStream by LocaliQ benchmarks put the average around $5 per click across industries. SEO has no click price, but it takes longer to start. ## Frequently asked questions

? How much does SEO cost per month for a small business?
Surveys of SEO providers show the most common retainer band is roughly $500 to $1,000 a month, with many small businesses paying up to a few thousand. Local SEO in a smaller market sits at the lower end; competitive or national campaigns sit higher.

? Is SEO a one-time cost?
No. A one-time audit or fix helps, but rankings depend on ongoing content, technical upkeep and authority. Competitors keep working too, so most businesses treat SEO as an ongoing channel.

? How long before SEO pays off?
Many sites see early movement in three to six months and stronger results in six to twelve months, depending on competition and how much work the site needs.

? Why do SEO prices vary so much?
Scope, competition, content volume and who does the work. Compare quotes by what is included each month, not by the headline price.
