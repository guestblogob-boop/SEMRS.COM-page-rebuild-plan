Dental SEO is how a dental practice shows up when nearby patients search for a dentist, a treatment or an emergency appointment. Unlike ads, the visits are not paid per click, and good rankings keep bringing patients month after month. This guide sets out eight steps and the patient privacy rules that apply to everything you publish.

## Key takeaways

- Most dental searches are local, so your Google Business Profile and reviews come first.
- Give every main treatment its own helpful page, written for patients, not search engines.
- Never confirm a patient relationship in review replies or use patient stories without written authorization.

## Why dental SEO matters

Patients search for "dentist near me", "emergency dentist open Saturday" and "dental implants cost [city]". Google's own SEO Starter Guide says creating content people find compelling and useful is likely to influence search presence more than any other single suggestion. For a practice, that means clear, honest pages that answer patients' questions.

## Dental SEO in 8 steps

@img chart

### 1. Optimize your Google Business Profile

Choose "Dentist" as the main category and add services such as emergency dental care or cosmetic dentistry where they fit. Add hours, insurance accepted, accessibility details and real photos. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/google-business-profile-optimization/) covers every field.

### 2. Build one page per treatment

Create pages for cleanings, fillings, implants, aligners, whitening, crowns, children's dentistry and emergencies. Each page should explain what the treatment is, who it suits, what to expect, recovery, price ranges or payment options, and how to book.

### 3. Use local keywords naturally

Include your town or neighborhood in page titles and headings where it reads naturally, such as "Dental Implants in Plano". Our guide to [keyword research](/keyword-research/) explains how to choose them.

### 4. Earn and answer reviews

Ask happy patients for reviews with a direct link. Reply politely without confirming they are a patient or mentioning treatment details.

### 5. Make the site fast and mobile-friendly

Many patients search on phones. Keep pages light, put the phone number at the top and make online booking easy.

### 6. Add dental schema

Structured data for a dentist (Dentist or MedicalBusiness types) tells search engines your address, hours and services. Our free [local business schema generator](/tools/local-business-schema-generator/) helps.

### 7. Publish helpful patient guides

Answer common questions: "How long do implants last?", "Is teeth whitening safe?", "What counts as a dental emergency?" Have a dentist review every article and add their name.

### 8. Earn local links and mentions

Sponsor local events, partner with schools and businesses, and keep your details consistent on directories.

## Privacy and advertising rules

In the US, the HIPAA Privacy Rule generally requires a patient's written authorization before you use their health information in marketing, including identifiable testimonials and photos. The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule bans fake reviews and rewarding only positive ones. Avoid claims you cannot prove, such as "best dentist in town".

## SEO or ads?

SEO builds lasting visibility; ads bring patients faster for high-value treatments. Most practices do best with both. Our guide to [dental marketing](/dental-marketing/) covers ads, reminders and social media alongside SEO.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is dental SEO?
Dental SEO is improving a practice's website and Google Business Profile so it appears higher when local patients search for a dentist or treatment.

? How long does dental SEO take?
Profile and review improvements can help within weeks. New treatment pages and links usually take three to six months to show their full effect.

? Do dentists need a page for every treatment?
Yes, for the treatments you want more of. A dedicated page answers patients' questions and gives Google a clear page to rank for each search.

? Can dentists reply to reviews?
Yes, but replies should be general and never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention treatment details.
