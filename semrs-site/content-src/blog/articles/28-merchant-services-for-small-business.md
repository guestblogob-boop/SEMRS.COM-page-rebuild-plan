Merchant services are the tools and accounts that let your business accept card and digital payments, in person and online. Choosing a provider affects every sale you make, because fees come out of each transaction. This guide explains how card processing works, the main pricing models, real published fees and the checks to run before you sign.

## Key takeaways

- Compare the effective rate: total fees divided by total card sales, not the headline rate.
- Flat-rate providers are simple for small volumes; interchange-plus pricing often costs less as volume grows.
- Read contracts for terms, early termination fees and equipment leases before you sign.

## How card processing works

When a customer pays by card, several parties take part:

1. **Your payment terminal or checkout** sends the card details securely.
2. **The processor and acquiring bank** pass the transaction to the card network.
3. **The card network** (such as Visa or Mastercard) routes it to the customer's bank.
4. **The issuing bank** approves or declines it.
5. **Funds are settled** to your account, minus fees, usually within one to two business days.

Fees go to the card issuer (interchange), the network (assessments) and your provider (markup).

## Merchant services pricing models

| Model | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Flat rate | One percentage plus a fixed fee per sale | Small or new businesses that want simple pricing |
| Interchange-plus | The actual card costs plus a fixed markup | Growing businesses with steady volume |
| Tiered | Sales sorted into "qualified" and other tiers | Rarely the best value; hard to compare |
| Subscription | Monthly fee plus interchange and a small per-sale fee | High-volume businesses |

## Published fees from popular providers

These are standard US rates published on the providers' pricing pages in November 2026:

- **Square (Free plan):** 2.6% + 15¢ in person, 3.3% + 30¢ online, with no monthly fee.
- **Stripe:** 2.9% + 30¢ for online cards, 2.7% + 5¢ in person, plus extra for international cards and currency conversion.

On a $100 sale, that means about $2.75 to $3.60 in fees, depending on the provider and how the card is taken. Online and manually keyed payments usually cost more because the risk of fraud is higher. Larger businesses can often negotiate lower rates.

## Fees to look for in the contract

- **Monthly or account fees.**
- **Chargeback fees** when a customer disputes a payment.
- **PCI compliance fees** or non-compliance penalties.
- **Equipment costs:** buying a terminal is usually cheaper than a long lease.
- **Early termination fees** for leaving before the contract ends.
- **Minimum monthly processing** requirements.

Ask for a full fee schedule in writing. If a provider will not give one, look elsewhere.

## How to choose merchant services

1. **Work out your volume:** monthly card sales, average sale size and how many are online.
2. **Calculate your effective rate** from a recent statement: total fees divided by total card sales.
3. **Get quotes in the same format**, using your real numbers.
4. **Check what you need:** in-person terminals, online checkout, invoices, recurring billing.
5. **Check payout speed** and holds on funds.
6. **Prefer month-to-month terms** without long equipment leases.

## Security and compliance

Any business that takes cards must protect card data. The PCI Security Standards Council sets the PCI DSS rules for businesses that store, process or transmit card data. Most small businesses meet them by using a provider whose terminals and checkout keep card data off their own systems, and by completing the yearly self-assessment their provider asks for.

## Frequently asked questions

? What are merchant services?
Merchant services are the accounts, tools and processing that let a business accept card and digital payments. They include payment terminals, online checkout and the merchant account that receives the money.

? How much do merchant services cost a small business?
You usually pay a percentage of each card sale plus a small fixed fee. In November 2026, published flat rates from Square and Stripe ranged from 2.6% + 15¢ in person to 3.3% + 30¢ online. Check each provider's current rates and contract.

? Is flat-rate or interchange-plus pricing better?
Flat-rate pricing is simpler and often fine for small volumes. Interchange-plus usually costs less as volume grows, because you pay the real card costs plus a set markup.

? Can I avoid long merchant service contracts?
Yes. Many providers offer month-to-month terms with no early termination fee. Read the contract before signing, and avoid long equipment leases.
