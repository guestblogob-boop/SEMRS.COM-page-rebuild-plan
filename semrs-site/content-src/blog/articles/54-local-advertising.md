Local advertising helps a small business reach people who live or work nearby, the customers most likely to buy. The best mix depends on what you sell and how people find businesses like yours. This guide covers ten ideas, online and offline, how to track each one and how to pick the few that fit your budget.

## Key takeaways

- Start with free and low-cost channels: your Google Business Profile, reviews and local partnerships.
- Add paid channels that match intent: search and Local Services Ads for urgent needs, social and mail for awareness.
- Give every channel its own tracking number, link or code so you know what works.

## 10 local advertising ideas

@img chart

### Online

1. **Google Business Profile:** free, and often the first thing local customers see. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/google-business-profile-optimization/) covers every field.
2. **Local search ads:** reach people searching for your service in your area. See our guide to [Google Ads for small business](/google-ads-for-small-business/).
3. **Local Services Ads:** pay per lead with a Google badge in eligible categories. Our comparison of [Local Services Ads vs Google Ads](/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads/) explains how it works.
4. **Social ads by location:** show offers, events and before-and-after photos to people nearby.
5. **Local directories:** industry and city directories where your customers look.

### Offline

6. **Direct mail:** postcards to the streets you serve. Our guide to [direct mail marketing](/direct-mail-marketing/) covers USPS Every Door Direct Mail.
7. **Local sponsorships:** youth teams, school events and charity runs.
8. **Vehicle and shop signage:** your van and storefront work every day.
9. **Community events:** stalls at markets, open days and workshops.
10. **Partner referrals:** businesses that serve the same customers, such as a plumber and a kitchen fitter.

## Choose the right mix

| Your situation | Start with |
|---|---|
| Urgent services (plumbing, locksmith, towing) | Business Profile, search ads, Local Services Ads |
| Visual businesses (restaurants, salons, home design) | Business Profile, social ads, events |
| Neighborhood services (cleaning, lawn care) | Direct mail, Business Profile, referrals |
| New business | Business Profile, opening offer, community events |

## Track every local ad

- **Unique phone numbers** for each channel.
- **QR codes** that go to a page about the exact offer.
- **UTM links** for online ads; our free [UTM builder](/tools/utm-builder/) creates them.
- **Ask every new customer** how they found you.

Compare channels by cost per customer, not by clicks or reach. Our guide to setting an [advertising budget](/advertising-budget/) shows how.

## Common mistakes

- **Spreading a small budget across many channels.**
- **No tracking**, so you cannot tell what worked.
- **Ads that send people to the home page** instead of the offer.
- **Ignoring reviews**, which people check before calling.
- **Stopping too soon:** local awareness builds with repetition.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is the best local advertising for a small business?
For most businesses, a strong Google Business Profile with reviews, plus search ads for urgent needs or direct mail for neighborhood services. The right mix depends on how your customers look for you.

? Which local ads are free?
A Google Business Profile, other free directory listings, social media posts and partnerships with other local businesses cost little or nothing but time.

? How much should I spend on local ads?
Work back from what a customer is worth and how many you want each month, then test that budget for at least a month.

? How do I track offline advertising?
Use a unique phone number, QR code or offer code for each channel, and ask every new customer how they found you.
