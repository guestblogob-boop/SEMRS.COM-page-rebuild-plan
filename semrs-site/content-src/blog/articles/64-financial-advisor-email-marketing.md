Email lets financial advisors stay in touch with clients and prospects between meetings, explain market moves calmly and earn referrals. It is also regulated: many advisor emails count as communications with the public, with approval and recordkeeping rules. This guide covers financial advisor email marketing ideas, how to grow your list and the compliance basics.

## Key takeaways

- Send useful, regular emails: market context, planning reminders and answers to client questions.
- Under FINRA Rule 2210, an email sent to more than 25 retail investors within 30 days is a retail communication that needs principal approval before use.
- Keep records of every communication, and work with your compliance team before you send.

## Financial advisor email marketing: what to send

@img chart

| Email | Example | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Market context | What a rate change means for savers | When markets move |
| Planning reminders | Year-end tax and contribution deadlines | Seasonal |
| Educational series | Five emails on planning for retirement | For new subscribers |
| Client updates | Office news, new services, events | Monthly or quarterly |
| Answers to questions | "Should I pay off my mortgage early?" | Monthly |

Keep each email short, clear and balanced. Avoid promising returns or downplaying risk.

## Grow your list the right way

- **Offer a useful guide** on your website, such as a retirement checklist, in exchange for an email address.
- **Invite seminar and webinar attendees** to subscribe.
- **Ask clients to forward** emails to friends who might benefit.
- **Never buy email lists.**

Follow the FTC's CAN-SPAM rules for commercial email: honest headers and subject lines, your postal address and an easy unsubscribe.

## FINRA rules for advisor emails

FINRA Rule 2210 covers written, including electronic, communications. Key points:

- **Retail communication:** a communication distributed to **more than 25 retail investors within any 30-day period**. Emails to a larger list usually fall here.
- **Correspondence:** a communication sent to **25 or fewer** retail investors within 30 days, supervised under the firm's correspondence rules.
- **Principal approval:** a registered principal must approve each retail communication **before** it is used or filed, with some exceptions.
- **Filing:** some retail communications must be filed with FINRA, for example by new member firms during their first year.
- **Records:** firms must keep copies of retail communications, the dates of use and the approving principal's details.

Investment advisers registered with the SEC follow the SEC's Marketing Rule instead or as well. Your compliance team will know which rules apply.

## Frequently asked questions

? What should financial advisors send in emails?
Short, balanced, useful emails: market context, planning reminders, educational series and answers to common client questions.

? Do advisor emails need compliance approval?
Under FINRA Rule 2210, an email sent to more than 25 retail investors within 30 days is a retail communication that needs principal approval before use. Follow your firm's policies.

? How often should advisors email clients?
Monthly is a common rhythm, plus timely notes when markets move. Consistency matters more than volume.

? Can advisors buy email lists?
It is not recommended. Bought lists hurt deliverability and trust, and may break privacy and anti-spam rules.
