Google Ads for small business can bring calls and enquiries within days, because your ad shows when someone searches for exactly what you sell. You set the budget, pay only when someone clicks and can stop at any time. This beginner guide explains how billing works, how to plan a budget and how to build your first search campaign without wasting money.

## Key takeaways

- Start with one search campaign, a small list of high-intent keywords and one matching landing page.
- Set conversion tracking before you spend, so you judge ads by leads, not clicks.
- Your daily budget is an average: Google can spend up to twice it on a busy day but bills no more than 30.4 times it in a month.

## How Google Ads works

Google Ads is an auction. When someone searches, Google picks which ads to show and in what order. It looks at your bid, the quality of your ad and landing page, and how relevant they are to the search. You usually pay per click, so an ad that shows but gets no clicks costs nothing.

There are several campaign types. For a small business, start with **Search**. Search ads answer a need someone is typing right now, such as "emergency plumber Denver" or "tax accountant near me". Display, video and Performance Max campaigns can work later, once you know which searches bring customers.

## What Google Ads costs a small business

You choose the budget, so there is no minimum spend. What you pay per click depends on your industry and area. In the 2025 benchmarks, the average cost per click across industries was $5.26 and the average cost per lead was $70.11. Legal, home services and dental clicks cost more than average.

How billing works matters for planning. Google's help page explains that a campaign can spend up to twice its average daily budget on some days. Over a month, it will charge no more than 30.4 times the daily budget. So a $20 daily budget means about $608 a month at most.

## Google Ads for small business: your first campaign

@img chart

1. **Set up conversion tracking.** Track calls, form submissions and bookings. Without it, you cannot tell good keywords from bad ones.
2. **Choose a Search campaign** and turn off the Display Network and search partners at first.
3. **Set your location** to the area you actually serve, using "presence" (people in the area), not just interest.
4. **Pick 10 to 20 keywords** that show buying intent, such as "boiler repair Leeds" or "same day dentist Austin".
5. **Add negative keywords** such as "jobs", "free", "DIY" and "course" so you do not pay for the wrong searches.
6. **Write three ads** per ad group with the service, the area and a clear next step.
7. **Send clicks to a matching page.** A plumber's "boiler repair" ad should land on a boiler repair page with a phone number at the top.
8. **Set a daily budget** you can sustain for at least a month, so Google has time to learn.

## Keywords and match types

| Match type | How it works | Example keyword | Can show for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact | Same meaning as the keyword | [roof repair dallas] | "dallas roof repair" |
| Phrase | Includes the meaning of the phrase | "roof repair dallas" | "emergency roof repair dallas" |
| Broad | Related searches | roof repair dallas | "roofers near me" |

Start with exact and phrase match. Broad match can work with smart bidding and good conversion data, but it spends faster on loosely related searches. Check the search terms report every week and add any irrelevant terms as negatives.

## Common beginner mistakes to avoid

- **No conversion tracking.** You end up optimizing for clicks that never call.
- **Too many keywords at once.** The budget spreads thin and nothing gets enough data.
- **Sending everyone to the home page.** Each ad needs a page about that exact service.
- **Accepting every recommendation.** Some suggestions raise spend without raising leads. Read each one first.
- **Changing everything daily.** Campaigns need one to two weeks of data before big changes.
- **Ignoring the phone.** If calls go to voicemail, the best ads in the world will not help.

## How to measure results

Judge your ads by cost per lead and cost per customer, not clicks. Each month, check which keywords brought leads, which ads won and how many leads became customers. If you want to know your return, our free [ROAS calculator](/tools/roas-calculator/) compares ad spend with revenue.

Give a new campaign about 30 days before you decide it does not work. The first weeks are for learning which searches convert.

## Frequently asked questions

? How much should a small business spend on Google Ads?
There is no minimum. Many small businesses start with $10 to $50 a day for one campaign, then raise the budget for keywords that bring leads. Plan for a full month at your chosen level so the campaign has time to learn.

? Is Google Ads worth it for a small business?
It usually is when people search for what you sell and a customer is worth more than the cost of winning one. It is less useful for products nobody searches for yet. Track leads from day one so you know.

? Can I advertise on Google for free?
You cannot run Google Ads for free, but a Google Business Profile is free and shows your business in Maps and local results. Many local businesses get most of their calls from it.

? How long does Google Ads take to work?
Ads can show within hours of approval, and leads can come in the first week. Expect the first month to be mostly testing before results settle.
