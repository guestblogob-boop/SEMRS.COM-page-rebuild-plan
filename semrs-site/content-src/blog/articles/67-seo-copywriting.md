SEO copywriting is writing web pages that answer a searcher's question clearly and persuade them to act, while making the topic easy for search engines to understand. Good copy is written for people first. Search engines reward pages that help readers, so clear, useful copy and good rankings usually go together.

## Key takeaways

- Write for the reader first: answer the question near the top, in plain words.
- Use the words your customers use in the title, the first lines and one or two subheadings, then write naturally.
- Google's own guidance warns against keyword stuffing and "search engine-first" content.

[glance: SEO copywriting at a glance | Answer the question first | Use customers' words | Clear title and headings | One clear next step]

## What SEO copywriting means

SEO and copywriting have different jobs. SEO helps the right page get found. Copywriting turns a visit into an enquiry or a sale. SEO copywriting does both on the same page.

Google says it focuses on content created to help people, not content made mainly to rank. Its guide asks whether a reader will leave "feeling like they've had a satisfying experience". That is a copywriting question as much as an SEO one.

## The 7 rules to follow

1. **Start with the search question.** Look at how people phrase it, then answer it in the first two or three sentences.
2. **Use your customers' words.** Read reviews, emails and sales calls. If customers say "leaky roof", don't write "roofing remediation".
3. **Write a clear title.** Google advises descriptive, concise title text. Put the topic near the start.
4. **Plan the headings.** Each subheading should cover one idea and read like a question or a promise.
5. **Prove what you say.** Add prices, examples, steps, photos of real work and sources.
6. **Keep it easy to scan.** Short paragraphs, lists, tables and plain words.
7. **End with one next step.** A quote form, a call or a booking link, not five choices.

## Anatomy of a well-written page

@img chart

Every strong page has the same parts. The title promises an answer, the first lines give it, the headings guide the reader and the call to action tells them what to do next.

| Page part | What good copy does | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Title tag | Names the topic and the benefit | "Home" or a list of keywords |
| Meta description | Says what the reader gets | Copy of the first paragraph |
| First paragraph | Answers the question directly | A long story before the point |
| Subheadings | One idea each, easy to scan | Vague labels like "More info" |
| Body | Facts, steps, examples, prices | Filler and repeated phrases |
| Call to action | One clear next step | Several competing buttons |

## Keywords without keyword stuffing

Keywords still matter, because they tell you what people want. Use the main phrase in the title, the first lines and one or two subheadings. After that, write naturally and use related words.

Google's spam policies describe keyword stuffing as filling a page with keywords or numbers to manipulate rankings. Repeating a phrase in every sentence reads badly and can hurt the page. Our guide to [keyword research](/keyword-research/) shows how to choose the right phrase for each page.

## Website copywriting for SEO: pages that sell

Service and product pages need more than information. They should also answer the doubts that stop people from buying.

- **Who it is for:** name the customer and the problem.
- **What happens:** the steps, from first call to finished job.
- **What it costs:** a price, a range or what affects the price.
- **Why trust you:** reviews, licenses, guarantees and real examples.
- **What to do next:** one button with a clear label.

## Check your copy before you publish

- Does the first paragraph answer the search question?
- Would a customer understand every word?
- Is every claim true and, where needed, sourced?
- Can a reader scan the page and still get the point?
- Is there one clear call to action?

It is writing web pages that answer what people search for and persuade them to act, while making the topic clear to search engines.

? Is SEO copywriting still important?
Yes. Search engines aim to reward helpful pages, and good copy is what makes a page helpful and persuasive.

? How many times should I use a keyword?
There is no magic number. Use it in the title, the opening and one or two headings, then write naturally with related words.

? What is the difference between SEO writing and copywriting?
SEO writing focuses on being found and understood. Copywriting focuses on persuading the reader. Good web copy combines both.
