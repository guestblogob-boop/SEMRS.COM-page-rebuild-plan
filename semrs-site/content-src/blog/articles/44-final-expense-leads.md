Final expense leads are people, usually older adults, who have asked about small whole life policies that cover funeral and end-of-life costs. Many agents buy these leads, but quality varies widely, and the rules on calling and texting are strict. This guide compares bought and self-generated leads, explains how to judge a vendor and shows seven ways to build your own.

## Key takeaways

- Ask every lead vendor whether leads are exclusive, how fresh they are and how consent was collected.
- Your own leads, from referrals, local search and community events, cost less over time and convert better.
- Follow Do Not Call rules, calling hours and consent rules for texts and automated calls.

## Types of final expense leads

| Lead type | How it works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Internet leads | People fill in an online form | Often shared; speed matters |
| Direct mail leads | People return a reply card | Slower but often more engaged |
| Live transfers | A call center connects an interested person | Higher cost; check consent and scripts |
| Aged leads | Older leads sold cheaply | Low contact rates; check Do Not Call |
| Your own leads | Referrals, search, events | Take time to build |

## How to judge a lead vendor

@img chart

1. **Exclusive or shared?** Shared leads go to several agents at once.
2. **How fresh?** Ask how quickly leads are delivered after the form is filled in.
3. **How was consent collected?** Ask to see the form wording and records.
4. **Return policy:** can you return wrong numbers and fake details?
5. **Test small:** buy a small batch and track how many become policies.

Compare vendors by cost per policy sold, not cost per lead.

## 7 ways to build your own leads

1. **Ask for referrals** at policy delivery and at annual reviews.

## Contact rules every agent must follow

**Do Not Call.** The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule requires sellers to check their calling lists against the National Do Not Call Registry at least every 31 days and to call only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. local time. An established business relationship exception lasts 18 months after a purchase or three months after an inquiry.

**Texts and automated calls.** Marketing texts and robocalls generally need the person's prior express written consent. Keep records of how and when each lead gave it.

**Honest selling to seniors.** Explain waiting periods, premiums and benefits clearly, and never pressure a prospect. State insurance departments regulate insurance advertising and sales practices.

## Frequently asked questions

? What are final expense leads?
They are people, usually older adults, who have asked for information about small whole life policies that pay for funeral and end-of-life costs.

? Are bought leads worth it?
They can be if they are fresh, exclusive and properly consented. Test a small batch and compare the cost per policy with your own lead sources.

? What is a good contact rate?
It varies by lead type, age and how fast you call. Track your own contact and close rates by vendor to compare them fairly.

? Can I text final expense leads?
Only with the person's prior express written consent for marketing texts, and you must honor opt-outs. Check your state's rules as well.
