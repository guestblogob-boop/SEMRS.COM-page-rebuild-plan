How local search works

When someone searches for a service with local intent, such as "plumber near me", "dentist in Bristol" or "accountant Toronto", Google shows a map with nearby businesses and a list of websites. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance (how well a business matches the search), distance (how close it is) and prominence (how well known and well reviewed it is). You cannot change your distance from a searcher, but you can improve relevance and prominence.

The local SEO essentials

  • A complete, verified Google Business Profile with the right categories, hours, photos and services.
  • Consistent business details: the same name, address and phone number on your website, profile and directories.
  • A steady flow of genuine reviews, with thoughtful replies.
  • Clear service and location pages on your website, written for real customers.
  • Structured data that states your business details precisely.

Local SEO tools for local businesses

The Google Review Link Generator creates a one-tap link to your review form, plus email and text templates, so asking every customer for a review becomes routine. For your website, the Local Business Schema Generator produces LocalBusiness markup with your address, hours and service areas, and the SERP Snippet Preview helps you write local page titles that fit and get clicked.

Local SEO in the US, UK, Canada and Australia

The principles are the same in every country, but the details differ: directories (Yelp in the US, Yell in the UK, Yellow Pages in Canada and Australia), address formats and spelling. Write the way your local customers search, list only areas you genuinely serve, and never use fake addresses or virtual offices to appear in other towns; Google suspends profiles that do.

Google explains how local results are ranked in Tips to improve your local ranking on Google.

Frequently asked questions

What matters most for local rankings?

A complete and accurate Google Business Profile, genuine reviews, consistent business details and a website that clearly explains your services and area.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews matters more than a one-off burst.

Should I create a page for every town I serve?

Only for areas where you genuinely work and have something specific to say. Near-identical town pages are treated as doorway pages.
