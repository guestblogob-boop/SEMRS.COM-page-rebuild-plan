Google Ads keywords decide which searches can trigger your ads, so they decide where your budget goes. Good keywords bring people ready to call, book or buy. Poor ones spend money on browsers, job seekers and people looking for free advice. This guide shows how to choose keywords, use match types and negative keywords, and keep improving your list.

## Key takeaways

- Choose keywords with buying intent, such as a service plus a location or "price", "quote" or "book".
- Start with phrase and exact match, then widen only when you have conversion data.
- Negative keywords do not cover close variants, so add plurals and synonyms yourself.

## What makes a good Google Ads keyword

The best keywords match what you sell, where you sell it and when someone is ready to act.

| Intent | Example | Good for ads? |
|---|---|---|
| Buying | emergency plumber Austin | Yes, high intent |
| Comparing | best accountant for small business | Often, with a strong page |
| Researching | how to fix a leaking tap | Rarely; better for blog content |
| Not a customer | plumber jobs, plumbing course | No; add as negatives |

## How to choose Google Ads keywords in 7 steps

@img chart

1. **List your services and locations.** Each service you want leads for needs its own set of keywords.
2. **Add buying words.** Price, cost, quote, near me, book, hire, same day and emergency.
3. **Expand with Keyword Planner.** It suggests related keywords, monthly searches and typical bids. Our [Google Keyword Planner guide](/google-keyword-planner/) explains how to use it.
4. **Check the bids against your budget.** If a click costs more than you can afford, use more specific phrases.
5. **Group keywords tightly.** One service per ad group, with ads and a landing page about that service.
6. **Choose match types** (below).
7. **Add negative keywords** before launch.

## Keyword match types

Google's help page lists three match types:

| Match type | How to write it | When ads can show |
|---|---|---|
| Broad match | roof repair | Related searches, including ones without your exact words |
| Phrase match | "roof repair" | Searches that include the meaning of your keyword |
| Exact match | [roof repair] | Searches with the same meaning or intent |

Broad match is the default. It reaches the most searches but spends fastest on loosely related terms. For small budgets, start with phrase and exact match. Try broad match later with smart bidding once conversion tracking has enough data.

## Negative keywords

Negative keywords stop your ads from showing on searches that contain them. Google's help page notes that negative keywords do not match close variants, so you need to add synonyms and singular or plural forms yourself.

Common negatives for service businesses:

- jobs, careers, salary, hiring
- free, DIY, how to, tutorial
- course, training, school, certification
- used, second hand (if you only sell new)
- competitor names you do not want to pay for

## Use the search terms report every week

The search terms report shows the real searches that triggered your ads. Each week:

- **Add good searches** as new keywords.
- **Add irrelevant searches** as negatives.
- **Pause keywords** that spend money but never convert.

This is where most savings come from in the first months.

## Keyword mistakes that waste budget

- **Single broad words** such as "plumbing" or "insurance".
- **Too many keywords** in one ad group with one generic ad.
- **No location focus**, so ads show to people outside your area.
- **No negative list.**
- **Judging keywords by clicks**, not by leads and customers.

## Frequently asked questions

? How many keywords should a Google Ads campaign have?
For a small business, 10 to 20 well-chosen keywords per service is a good start. A short, focused list with good negatives beats a long list that spreads the budget thin.

? Which match type is best for small budgets?
Phrase and exact match usually waste less money at the start. Broad match can work later with smart bidding and reliable conversion tracking.

? Do negative keywords block plurals and misspellings?
Google's help page says negative keywords do not match close variants, so add synonyms and singular or plural versions yourself.

? How do I find keywords my competitors use?
Keyword Planner can suggest ideas from a competitor's page address. Searching your main services and reading the ads that appear also shows what others target.
