Quick business funding helps when a big order, a repair or a slow month needs cash within days, not weeks. Fast money usually costs more, and some offers hide their true cost behind daily payments and "factor rates". This guide compares the main options for quick business funding, shows how to work out the real cost and lists the warning signs that should make you walk away.

## Key takeaways

- The fastest options usually cost the most; a bank line of credit set up in advance is often the cheapest way to have money ready.
- Convert every offer to a total cost in dollars and an annual rate before you compare.
- Walk away from pressure, upfront fees, blank documents or offers that ignore your ability to repay.

## How to compare the real cost

Fast funding is often quoted in ways that make it look cheaper than it is:

- **Factor rates:** a factor rate of 1.3 on $20,000 means you repay $26,000. That $6,000 cost over a few months is far more expensive than it sounds.
- **Daily or weekly payments:** these can strain cash flow, even if the total seems manageable.
- **Fees:** origination, processing and early repayment fees all add up.

For every offer, write down:

1. **The amount you receive** after fees.
2. **The total you will repay.**
3. **How long you have**, and how often you pay.
4. **The cost in dollars** (total repaid minus amount received).
5. **Whether paying early saves money.**

Ask each lender for an annual percentage rate. If they will not give one, be cautious.

## Warning signs to walk away from

- Pressure to sign today.
- Fees you must pay before you receive the money.
- Blank spaces in documents you are asked to sign.
- No questions about your revenue or ability to repay.
- Unclear answers about the total cost.
- Requests to sign over control of your bank account without clear limits.

## Before you borrow: other ways to find cash

- **Collect faster:** send invoices the day work finishes and follow up quickly.
- **Ask suppliers for longer terms.**
- **Offer a small discount for early payment** to big customers.
- **Cut or pause** spending that does not bring revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is the fastest way to get business funding?
An existing line of credit or a business credit card is usually fastest. Online lenders and cash advances can fund in days, but usually cost much more.

? Is a merchant cash advance a loan?
It is usually structured as a purchase of your future card sales rather than a loan, so it may not come with the same disclosures. Its effective cost is often very high, so compare it carefully.

? How do I compare a factor rate with an interest rate?
Work out the total you repay, subtract what you receive and compare that dollar cost over the time you have to repay. Ask the lender for an annual percentage rate as well.

? Can I get quick business funding with bad credit?
Some lenders focus on revenue rather than credit, but costs are usually higher. Check the full cost and the repayment schedule before you sign.
