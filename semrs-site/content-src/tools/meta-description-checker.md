## What this checker tests

Most meta tag problems are simple, but they are easy to miss when you are writing dozens of pages. This checker runs the same checks an SEO specialist would run by hand, every time you type:

- **Title length and pixel width.** Titles around 30–60 characters, and under roughly 600 pixels wide, usually display in full on desktop.
- **Description length and pixel width.** Descriptions of about 120–155 characters give Google enough to show without being cut mid-sentence.
- **Keyword in the title,** and how close it is to the start. Searchers scan the first few words, and Google gives the title real weight when judging relevance.
- **Keyword in the description.** Google bolds words that match the search, which draws the eye to your result.
- **Spammy patterns.** All-caps titles and words repeated three or more times.
- **Clarity signals.** Whether the description ends in a complete sentence and includes a call to action.

## How to use the checker

1. Enter the **focus keyword**: the main phrase you want this page to be found for. Use one phrase per page.
2. Paste the **title tag** you plan to use.
3. Paste the **meta description**.
4. Optionally add the **page URL** so the tool can include canonical and Open Graph URL tags.
5. Work through the checklist. Green ticks pass; red crosses need fixing; amber marks are suggestions.
6. Click **Copy tags** and paste them into your page head, or copy the title and description into your SEO plugin fields.

The checks update as you type, so you can try several versions quickly. Your work is saved only in your own browser.

## Worked example

An accountant in Manchester has this on their tax return service page:

Title: "Services" (8 characters). Description: empty.

The checker flags a title that is far too short, a missing keyword and a missing description. Google would probably build a title from the page heading and pull random text for the snippet.

After two minutes of edits:

Title: "Self-Assessment Tax Returns in Manchester | Smith & Co" (54 characters). Description: "Fixed-fee self-assessment tax returns for sole traders and landlords in Manchester. Filed on time, HMRC queries handled. Get a quote today."

Now every check passes: the keyword sits at the start of the title, the city is included, and the description explains who it is for, what is included and what to do next.

## Writing titles that get clicked

A good title answers three questions in under 60 characters: what is this page, is it for me, and why this result rather than the next one? A useful pattern for service pages is **Service + location or audience + differentiator | Brand**. For guides and blog posts, a clear promise works better: "How to File a Self-Assessment Tax Return (2026 Guide)".

Avoid starting every title on your site with the brand name. On inner pages the brand adds little for searchers and pushes the useful words towards the cut-off point.

## Writing descriptions that earn the click

Think of the description as a short advert. State the benefit, add a specific detail that builds trust (a price from, a guarantee you really offer, opening hours, a service area) and finish with an action: get a quote, book online, read the guide. Write for people, not for search engines. Google does not use the meta description to rank pages, but a clearer description can win more clicks from the same position.

## Common mistakes the checker catches

- The same title or description on many pages, often left over from a theme or template.
- Keywords stuffed together with commas or pipes.
- Descriptions that stop mid-word because they are too long.
- Titles written for the business rather than the searcher ("Welcome to our website").
- Forgetting the keyword in the title altogether.

## Tips for local businesses

For businesses serving local customers in the US, UK, Canada or Australia, the location usually belongs in the title of each service page and in the description. Use the city or area name customers actually type, and the spelling they use ("neighbourhood" in the UK and Australia, "neighborhood" in the US). Add details that matter locally: emergency or same-day service, free parking, evening appointments, or the areas you cover. Make sure the phone number and address on the page match your Google Business Profile exactly.

## Frequently asked questions

Q: Is the meta description a ranking factor?
A: No. Google has said it does not use the meta description to rank pages. It matters because a clear, relevant description can increase the share of searchers who click your result.

Q: Should every page have a meta description?
A: Yes, every important page should have a unique one. If you leave it empty, Google writes its own from the page text, which is sometimes fine and sometimes awkward.

Q: How many keywords should I target in one title?
A: One main phrase per page, written naturally. Related words can appear in the description and on the page itself.

Q: What are Open Graph tags?
A: Open Graph tags control the title, description and image shown when your page is shared on social networks such as Facebook and LinkedIn. The checker generates them from the same text.

Q: Why does my SEO plugin show a different length limit?
A: Tools use slightly different limits because Google measures pixels, not characters, and display widths change over time. Treat any limit as a guide and check the visual preview.
