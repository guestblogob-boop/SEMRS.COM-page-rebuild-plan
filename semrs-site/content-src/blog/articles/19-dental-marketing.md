Dental marketing is how a practice gets found, trusted and booked by new patients, and how it keeps the patients it already has. Most new patients start with a search such as "dentist near me" and choose from the map results, reviews and the practice website. This guide sets out eight ways to win patients, what each costs in time and money, and the privacy and review rules every practice must follow.

## Key takeaways

- Your Google Business Profile, reviews and website decide most first bookings, so fix them before you buy ads.
- Use paid search for high-value treatments such as implants, Invisalign and emergency care, with one page per treatment.
- Patient privacy rules and the FTC review rule apply to all dental marketing, including social media.

## How patients choose a dentist

Patients look for three things: a practice close to home or work, good reviews and an easy way to book. Many also check whether you take their insurance, offer evening or weekend hours and treat nervous patients. If your profile and website answer those questions quickly, you win the booking.

## 8 ways to get new dental patients

@img chart

### 1. Complete your Google Business Profile

Choose "Dentist" as the main category and add services such as "Cosmetic dentist" or "Emergency dental service" where they fit. Add hours, insurance plans, accessibility details and real photos of your team and rooms. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/google-business-profile-optimization/) covers every field.

### 2. Ask every happy patient for a review

Reviews are one of the strongest local signals. Ask in person at checkout, then send a short text or email with a direct link. Our free [Google review link generator](/tools/google-review-link-generator/) makes that link in seconds. Reply to every review in a calm, general way, and never confirm that the reviewer is a patient.

### 3. Build a fast website with one page per treatment

Patients search for treatments, not for "services". Give implants, Invisalign, whitening, children's dentistry and emergency care their own pages. Each page should explain the treatment, what to expect, prices or payment options, and how to book. Keep the site fast on phones and accessible to everyone; our [ADA website compliance guide](/ada-website-compliance/) explains what that means.

### 4. Use local SEO and helpful content

Answer real questions in short articles: "How long does an implant take to heal?" or "What counts as a dental emergency?" Add your city to page titles where it reads naturally. Our guide to [SEO for small business](/seo-for-small-business/) explains the basics of dental SEO marketing step by step.

### 5. Run Google Ads for high-value treatments

Paid search works well for treatments with high value or urgent need. Keep it tight: one campaign per treatment, keywords with the city name, and ads that send people to the matching page. Dental clicks are among the more expensive in local advertising, so track booked appointments, not clicks. Our guide to [Google Ads cost](/google-ads-cost/) explains how to set a budget.

### 6. Make booking easy

Put the phone number at the top of every page and add online booking if your system allows it. Answer calls quickly, and call back missed calls the same day. Many new patients book with the first practice that answers.

### 7. Keep patients with email and text reminders

Recall reminders, hygiene appointments and simple newsletters keep chairs full. Get clear consent before you send marketing messages, and keep appointment reminders separate from promotions.

### 8. Show your team on social media

Short posts about your team, your community and simple care tips build trust. Never post patient photos, X-rays or stories without written authorization.

## Privacy and review rules for dental marketing

**Patient privacy.** In the US, the HIPAA Privacy Rule generally requires a patient's written authorization before you use their health information for marketing. That includes testimonials, before-and-after photos and stories that identify a patient. The [HHS marketing guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html) explains the rule.

**Reviews.** The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect on October 21, 2024. It bans fake reviews, buying positive reviews and hiding negative ones. You may ask for honest reviews; you may not reward only good ones. Staff reviews must disclose that the writer works for you.

**Claims.** Avoid "best dentist in town" or guaranteed results unless you can prove them. Some dental boards also limit how you describe specialties.

## How to measure what works

1. **Ask every new patient** how they found you and record it.
2. **Use call tracking** for ads and your Business Profile.
3. **Track bookings** from your website forms.
4. **Review monthly:** new patients by source, cost per new patient and recall rates.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is the best marketing for dentists?
For most practices, a complete Google Business Profile, steady reviews and a fast website with treatment pages bring the most new patients for the least money. Google Ads adds patients for high-value treatments once those basics are in place.

? How much should a dental practice spend on marketing?
It depends on your goals and market. Start by working out what a new patient is worth over a year, then decide how much you can pay to win one. Track cost per new patient by channel and move budget to what works.

? Can dentists use patient testimonials in marketing?
Yes, with care. In the US you generally need the patient's written authorization to use their health information in marketing, and testimonials must be real and not paid for being positive.

? Should a dental practice hire a marketing agency?
An agency makes sense when you lack the time or skills in-house. Ask for clear reporting on new patients, ownership of your ad accounts and website, and a written scope with no long lock-in.
