Insurance agency social media helps local agents stay known and trusted between renewals. People rarely think about insurance until a life event, such as a new car, a new home or a new baby. Regular, helpful posts keep your agency in mind when that moment comes. This guide covers where to post, what to post and the rules to respect.

! This article is general information, not legal or compliance advice. Insurance advertising rules vary by state and carrier; check yours before you post.

## Key takeaways

- Post to educate and to show the people behind the agency; sell in a small share of posts only.
- Every post about coverage must be accurate and approved under your state and carrier rules.
- Reviews and testimonials need permission, and paid or rewarded endorsements must be disclosed.

Start with one or two platforms you can keep up every week. A quiet page looks worse than no page.

A simple starting point is to give most of your posts to education and community, and fewer to offers: for example, 40% education, 30% community and team, 20% reviews and stories, and 10% offers. This is a suggestion, not a rule, so adjust the mix to what your audience responds to.

## 8 post ideas for insurance agencies

1. **Answer one question a week,** such as "Does renters insurance cover a stolen bike?" Keep answers general and point people to call you for their own situation.
2. **Life event checklists:** new driver, new home, new baby, new business.
3. **Seasonal tips:** storm preparation, winter driving, holiday travel.
4. **Meet the team** posts with real photos.
5. **Community support:** local teams, charities and events you sponsor.
6. **Client stories,** shared only with written permission.
7. **Myth versus fact** posts about common coverage misunderstandings.
8. **Short videos** of an agent explaining one term in under a minute.

## Compliance basics for insurance posts

- **Keep coverage claims accurate.** Don't promise that a policy covers something unless it does, and avoid words like "best" or "cheapest" you can't prove.
- **Follow your state and carrier rules.** Many carriers require approval of ads and social posts that mention their products.
- **Disclose rewarded reviews.** The FTC's Endorsement Guides apply when a reviewer gets something for their review.
- **Protect privacy.** Never post client details or reply to a claim question in public; move it to a private message or call.
- **Keep records** of what you posted and when.

## Frequently asked questions

? What should an insurance agency post on social media?
Helpful answers, life event checklists, seasonal tips, team and community posts, and a few offers.

? How often should insurance agents post?
Two to four times a week is a realistic goal for a small agency. Consistency matters more than volume.

? Can I share client reviews on social media?
Yes, with the client's permission. If you gave them anything for the review, disclose it.

? Do I need approval for insurance social media posts?
Often, yes. Many carriers and states have advertising rules, so check with your carrier and compliance contact.
