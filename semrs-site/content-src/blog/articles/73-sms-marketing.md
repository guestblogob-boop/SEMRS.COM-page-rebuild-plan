SMS marketing means sending offers, reminders and updates to customers by text message. Texts are read quickly, which makes them powerful, and that is also why the rules are strict. This guide covers the consent you need, the words that must stop your messages, quiet hours and examples of texts customers welcome.

! This article is general information about US rules, not legal advice. Check with a lawyer before you start texting customers.

## Key takeaways

- US rules require prior express written consent before you send marketing texts with automated systems.
- Replies such as "stop", "unsubscribe" or "cancel" revoke consent, and requests must be honored within 10 business days at most.
- Text only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the customer's time zone, and keep every message short and useful.

## Why businesses use SMS marketing

Texts suit messages that are short and timely: appointment reminders, flash offers, back-in-stock alerts and order updates. They work best alongside email, which suits longer content. Many email and SMS marketing platforms now handle both from one contact list.

## The 7 rules for marketing texts

1. **Get written consent first.** The customer must clearly agree to receive marketing texts, for example by ticking an unticked box or texting a keyword. Never make consent a condition of buying.
2. **Say what they signed up for.** Your brand name, the type of messages, how often and that message and data rates may apply.
3. **Honor every opt-out.** Under the FCC rules, replies such as "stop", "quit", "end", "revoke", "opt out", "cancel" or "unsubscribe" revoke consent. Other words count too if a reasonable person would read them as a request to stop.
4. **Act on opt-outs quickly.** The rules say within a reasonable time, not more than ten business days.
5. **Respect quiet hours.** Telephone solicitations may not be made before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m., local time for the person you are contacting.
6. **Register your number.** US carriers require businesses to register before sending application-to-person texts from a standard local number (10DLC). Your SMS platform guides you through it.
7. **Keep records.** Store when and how each person gave consent.

## What a good marketing text looks like

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| Brand name | "Bright Smile Dental:" |

| Link | A short link to the booking page |

Keep it under 160 characters where you can, so it arrives as one message.

## Text examples customers welcome

- **Reminders:** "Your table for 4 is tonight at 7. Reply C to cancel."
- **Back in stock:** "The blue trail shoes are back in your size. Shop now: link"
- **Loyalty:** "Thanks for being a regular. Show this text for a free coffee this week."
- **Last chance:** "Our spring sale ends at midnight. Reply STOP to opt out."

## Email and SMS marketing together

|---|---|
| Long content, newsletters, guides | Short, urgent messages |
| Read when convenient | Usually read within minutes |
| Images and several links | One link, plain text |
| Unsubscribe link in the footer | STOP reply |

## Frequently asked questions

? Is SMS marketing legal?
Yes, with the right consent. US rules require prior express written consent for marketing texts sent with automated systems, and every opt-out must be honored.

? What words must stop my texts?
The FCC rules name "stop", "quit", "end", "revoke", "opt out", "cancel" and "unsubscribe", and any reply a reasonable person would understand as a request to stop.

? How quickly must I honor an opt-out?
Within a reasonable time, and no more than ten business days from the request.

? What time can I send marketing texts?
Between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the customer's local time. Many businesses stick to daytime hours on weekdays.
