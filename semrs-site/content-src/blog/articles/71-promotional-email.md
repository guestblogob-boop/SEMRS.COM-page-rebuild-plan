A promotional email is a message that advertises an offer, a product or an event to people on your list. Done well, it is one of the cheapest ways to bring back past customers. Done badly, it lands in spam or breaks the law. This guide shows five promotional email examples and the rules every sender must follow.

## Key takeaways

- Every offer email needs an honest subject line, your postal address and a clear way to opt out.
- Opt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days under the US CAN-SPAM Act.
- Gmail asks bulk senders to authenticate their mail and keep spam rates below 0.3%.

[glance: Promotional email at a glance | Honest subject line | Postal address | Easy opt-out | One clear offer]

## What makes an offer email work

People decide in seconds. The best ones do four things:

- **A clear subject line** that says what the offer is.
- **One main offer,** not five competing ones.
- **A short body** with the benefit, the deadline and any conditions.
- **One button** that leads to a page about that exact offer.

## The legal rules for marketing email in the US

The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists the main requirements for commercial email:

1. **Don't use false or misleading header information.** "From" and "Reply-To" must identify you.
2. **Don't use deceptive subject lines.**
3. **Identify the message as an ad,** clearly.
4. **Include your valid physical postal address.**
5. **Tell people how to opt out,** in a way that is easy to see and use.
6. **Honor opt-outs within 10 business days,** and keep the opt-out working for at least 30 days after sending.
7. **Monitor anyone who sends email for you.** You stay responsible.

The FTC notes that each separate email in violation can face penalties of up to $53,088. Other countries have their own rules, and many require consent before you send.

## Get into the inbox: sender rules

Large mailbox providers set their own rules. Google's email sender guidelines ask every sender to set up SPF or DKIM, and to keep spam rates reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail must also meet stricter rules, including one-click unsubscribe for marketing messages.

- **Authenticate your domain** (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) through your email platform.
- **Send only to people who asked** for your emails.
- **Remove addresses that bounce** or never open.
- **Don't buy lists.**

## Pre-send checklist

- Subject line matches the offer.
- Offer, deadline and conditions are clear.
- Postal address and unsubscribe link are in the footer.
- Links work and lead to the right page.
- It reads well on a phone.
- It was sent to a test inbox first.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is a promotional email?
An email whose main purpose is to advertise a product, service, offer or event.

? Do marketing emails need an unsubscribe link?
Yes. Under CAN-SPAM, every commercial email must explain how to opt out, and you must honor requests within 10 business days.

? How often should I send offer emails?
Often enough to stay remembered, but not so often that people unsubscribe. Many small businesses send one to four a month and watch unsubscribe rates.

? Why do my emails go to spam?
Common causes are missing authentication, sending to people who didn't sign up, misleading subject lines and high complaint rates.
