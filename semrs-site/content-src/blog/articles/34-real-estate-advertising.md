Real estate advertising works best when it builds your name in one area and gives buyers and sellers a reason to contact you, not just another listing to scroll past. This guide covers eight ideas that work for agents and brokers, how to measure them, and the fair housing and ad targeting rules every real estate ad must follow.

## Key takeaways

- Focus on one or two areas and become the agent people see everywhere there: search, social, mail and local events.
- Advertise to sellers with valuation offers and local market updates; advertise to buyers with useful area guides.
- Housing ads must follow the Fair Housing Act, and ad platforms limit targeting for housing in the US and Canada.

## 8 real estate advertising ideas that work

@img chart

### 1. A Google Business Profile with reviews

Many people search "real estate agent near me" or "realtor in [town]". A complete profile with recent reviews puts you in the map results. Our guide on how to get more [Google reviews](/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) shows how to ask within the rules.

### 2. Area guide pages on your website

Write a page for each neighborhood you serve: schools, parks, commute times, typical home types and recent market trends. These pages rank for local searches and show you know the area.

### 3. Seller offers: home valuations

A "What is my home worth?" page with a simple form attracts future sellers, who are usually the most valuable leads. Follow up personally with a real comparative market analysis.

### 4. Video tours and market updates

Short listing tours and monthly market update videos work well on social media and YouTube. Keep them under two minutes and show your face.

### 5. Search ads for seller and buyer intent

Search ads for phrases such as "sell my house [town]" reach people at the right moment. Send each ad to a matching page. Our guide to [Google Ads for small business](/google-ads-for-small-business/) covers the setup.

### 6. Social ads in your area

Social ads can reach people in your area with listing videos, open house dates and market updates. Use the housing ad category the platform requires.

### 7. Direct mail to a farm area

Postcards with just-sold results and market updates, sent to the same streets every month, build recognition. Add a QR code to your valuation page to track response.

### 8. Partners and community

Mortgage brokers, home inspectors, builders and local businesses can refer clients. Sponsoring local events keeps your name visible.

## Real estate advertising rules

**Fair housing.** The Fair Housing Act makes it illegal to publish any housing ad that shows a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin. Many states add more protected classes. Describe the property and area, not the people you think should live there.

**Ad targeting limits.** Google's personalized advertising policy says housing ads in the US and Canada cannot target by age, gender, parental status, marital status or ZIP code. Meta has a similar special ad category for housing. Plan campaigns around areas and interests that are allowed.

**License and brokerage rules.** Most states require your brokerage name in ads, and some require your license number. Use "Realtor" only if you are a member of the National Association of Realtors.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is the best real estate advertising for agents?
Most agents do best by owning one area: a strong Business Profile with reviews, area guide pages, a home valuation offer, and steady social and mail campaigns in the same neighborhoods.

? How much should a real estate agent spend on advertising?
It depends on your market and goals. Start with low-cost channels such as your profile and website, then add paid ads and track the cost per client before you increase spend.

? Can real estate ads target by age or ZIP code?
On Google in the US and Canada, housing ads cannot target by age, gender, parental status, marital status or ZIP code. Meta also requires a special ad category for housing.

? What words should real estate ads avoid?
Avoid words that describe who should live in a home rather than the home itself, such as references to religion, family type, age or ethnicity. Describe features, location and amenities instead.
