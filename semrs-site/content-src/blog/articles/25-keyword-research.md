Keyword research is how you find the words your customers type into Google before they buy. It tells you which pages to write, what to call them and which questions to answer. You do not need expensive tools to start. This guide walks through a six-step process for beginners, using free tools and your own customer knowledge.

## Key takeaways

- Start from your services and real customer questions, then use tools to expand and check them.
- Match each keyword to the search intent behind it: learning, comparing or buying.
- Give every page one main keyword, and never target the same keyword on two pages.

## What keyword research is and why it matters

A keyword is the phrase someone searches for, such as "emergency plumber Denver" or "how much does a website cost". Keyword research finds the phrases that bring the right visitors, not just the most visitors. A local bakery gains little from ranking for "bread", but a lot from "custom birthday cakes Austin".

Good research saves months of writing pages nobody searches for. It also shapes your site structure: each important search gets its own page.

## A 6-step process

@img chart

### 1. List your seed topics

Write down your services, products, locations and the questions customers ask on calls. Read your emails and reviews. Customers' own words are often better keywords than the words used inside your business.

### 2. Expand your list with free tools

- **Google search suggestions:** type a seed word and note the suggestions and the "People also ask" box.
- **Google Search Console:** the Performance report shows the searches that already bring people to your site, with clicks, impressions and average position.
- **Google Keyword Planner:** shows estimated monthly searches and ideas. It sits inside Google Ads and needs account setup with billing details, but you do not have to run ads.
- **The SEMRS [keyword ideas generator](/tools/keyword-ideas-generator/):** a free tool that expands one seed into many related ideas.

### 3. Check the search intent

Search the keyword yourself and look at the top results. They show what Google thinks people want.

| Intent | Example | Best page type |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | how to unclog a drain | Guide or article |
| Commercial | best CRM for small business | Comparison or buying guide |
| Transactional | book plumber near me | Service page with a clear call to action |
| Local | dentist in Leeds | Service page plus Google Business Profile |

If the top results are all guides, a sales page will struggle to rank, and the reverse is true too.

### 4. Judge difficulty honestly

Look at who ranks now. If the first page is full of national brands and big publishers, pick a more specific phrase. Longer phrases, such as "SEO for dental practices in Ohio", usually have fewer searches but less competition and clearer intent.

### 5. Map one keyword to each page

Give every page one main keyword and a few close variations. Two pages targeting the same keyword compete with each other. A simple spreadsheet works: keyword, intent, page address and status.

### 6. Track and update

After publishing, watch the page in Search Console. If it gets impressions for phrases you did not plan, add a section that answers them. Our guide to [SEO for small business](/seo-for-small-business/) shows how keyword work fits the rest of SEO.

## SEO keyword research mistakes to avoid

- **Chasing volume only.** A small, specific keyword that brings buyers beats a big one that brings browsers.
- **Ignoring intent.** Writing a blog post for a keyword where people want to buy rarely works.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating the phrase makes pages worse. Use it naturally, with synonyms.
- **One page for everything.** Each service and location deserves its own page.
- **Never revisiting.** Search habits change, and AI answers now cover many simple questions.

## Keywords for AI search

AI answers in Google and chat tools draw on pages that clearly answer specific questions. Question keywords, such as "how long does a roof replacement take", are worth a clear, direct answer near the top of a page. ## Frequently asked questions

? What is keyword research in SEO?
It is finding the phrases people search for that match what you offer, then choosing which ones each page should target. It guides what you write and how you structure your site.

? Can I do keyword research for free?
Yes. Google's search suggestions, Search Console and Keyword Planner are free to use, and the SEMRS keyword ideas generator is free too. Paid tools add more data, but beginners can get far without them.

? How many keywords should a page target?
One main keyword per page, plus close variations and related questions that fit naturally. Do not target the same main keyword on two pages.

? How often should I do keyword research?
Do a full round when you plan new pages, and check Search Console every month or two for new questions people use to find you.
