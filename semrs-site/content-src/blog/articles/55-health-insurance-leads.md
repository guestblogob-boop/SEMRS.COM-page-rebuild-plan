Health insurance leads are people who have asked for help choosing a health plan, whether through the Marketplace, an employer group or an individual policy. Agents can buy them or build their own. Either way, the rules are strict: Marketplace agents must document consent, and calls and texts have their own rules. This guide compares the options, shows seven ways to build your own leads and explains the consent rules.

## Key takeaways

- Bought leads are fast but often shared; your own leads cost less over time and trust you more.
- Marketplace agents must document a consumer's consent before helping, and document that the consumer reviewed the application.
- Follow Do Not Call rules and get written consent before marketing texts.

## Consent rules for Marketplace agents

@img chart

CMS's model consent form for Marketplace agents and brokers explains that agents, brokers and web-brokers must document a consumer's consent before helping them enroll, and must document that the consumer reviewed and confirmed their eligibility application before it is submitted.

The model form covers:

- **Who** gives consent and **who** receives it, including the agent's National Producer Number.
- **What** the agent may do: search for an application, complete it, help with ongoing maintenance and respond to Marketplace inquiries.
- **How** personal information will be used and protected.
- **How long** consent lasts and how to revoke it.

CMS says consent can be documented in different formats, such as a recorded call, text, email, an electronic document with a digital signature or a signed paper form.

## Buying vs building leads

| | Bought leads | Your own leads |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Immediate | Takes time |
| Exclusivity | Often shared | Yours alone |
| Consent records | Depend on the vendor | You control them |
| Cost over time | Pay for every lead | Falls as channels grow |

## 7 ways to build your own health insurance leads

1. **Referrals** from clients, accountants and HR advisers.
2. **Google Business Profile** with reviews, so local searchers find you.
3. **Helpful content** that explains plan types, deductibles and enrollment dates.
4. **Employer outreach** for small group plans.
5. **Community workshops** on choosing a plan, without pressure selling.
6. **Search ads** for clear-intent phrases in your licensed states.
7. **Year-round email** with reminders before enrollment periods.

## Call and text rules

The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule requires sellers to check their call lists against the National Do Not Call Registry at least every 31 days and to call only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. local time. Marketing texts and automated calls generally need prior express written consent. Keep records for every lead.

## Frequently asked questions

? What are health insurance leads?
They are people who have asked for help choosing or buying health coverage, such as a Marketplace plan, an individual policy or a small group plan.

? Do Marketplace agents need written consent?
Agents must document the consumer's consent before helping them enroll. CMS allows several formats, including recorded calls, texts, emails, electronic signatures and signed paper forms.

? Are bought leads worth it?
They can be if they are fresh, exclusive and properly consented. Test small and compare the cost per policy with your own sources.

? How can agents get free health insurance leads?
Referrals, a Google Business Profile, helpful content and community workshops cost little money but take steady effort.
