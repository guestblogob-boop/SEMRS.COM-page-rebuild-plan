Google reviews help local customers choose you, and they help your business show up in the map results. The good news is that most happy customers will leave a review if you ask at the right moment and make it easy. This guide covers seven ways to get more reviews, how to reply, and the rules from Google and the FTC that you must follow.

## Key takeaways

- Ask every customer, at the moment they are happiest, with a direct review link or QR code.
- Never pay, give discounts for reviews or ask only happy customers; Google and the FTC ban it.
- Reply to every review, good or bad, calmly and without sharing private details.

## Why Google reviews matter

Google's local ranking help page says prominence is one of the main factors in local results, and the number of reviews and their ratings are part of it. Reviews also influence the click: people compare stars, read recent comments and check how the owner replies.

## 7 ways to get more Google reviews

@img chart

### 1. Ask every customer

The simplest step is the one most businesses skip. Google's own tips say to remind customers to leave reviews. Make it part of your process, not an afterthought.

### 2. Ask at the right moment

Ask when the job is done and the customer is pleased: when a repair is finished, at checkout, or after a good follow-up visit.

### 3. Send a direct review link

A direct link opens the review box straight away. Our free [Google review link generator](/tools/google-review-link-generator/) creates one for your business. Send it by text or email on the same day.

### 4. Use a QR code in person

Put a QR code on receipts, invoices, the counter or a small card. Google's tips mention customers can scan a QR code to leave a review.

### 5. Make it part of your follow-up emails

Add a short review request to your thank-you email. Our guide to [marketing automation software](/marketing-automation-software/) shows how to send it automatically after each job.

### 6. Train your team

Staff who know why reviews matter ask naturally. Give them a short script: "If you were happy with us today, a Google review really helps a small business like ours."

### 7. Reply to every review

Replies show future customers you care. Google's tips recommend replying to reviews. Thank people by name if they used it, and answer complaints calmly with a way to talk offline.

## What Google and the FTC do not allow

| Not allowed | Why |
|---|---|
| Offering discounts, gifts or payment for reviews | Google bans incentives for reviews |
| Asking only happy customers to review | Google bans selectively soliciting positive reviews |
| Reviews from staff, family or yourself | Conflicts of interest are banned; FTC rules need disclosure for insiders |
| Buying fake reviews | The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule bans fake reviews |
| Pressuring people to change or remove negative reviews | Both Google and the FTC prohibit it |

Google's policy on fake engagement says businesses must not offer incentives or selectively ask for positive reviews. The FTC's rule, in force since October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews and rewards tied to positive reviews.

## How to handle negative Google reviews

- **Reply calmly** within a few days. Apologize for the experience without admitting legal fault.
- **Take it offline:** give a name and a phone number or email.
- **Do not share private details**, especially for health, legal or financial businesses.
- **Fix the cause** if the complaint is fair.
- **Report reviews** that break Google's policies, such as spam, offensive content or reviews from people who were never customers. You can flag them from your Business Profile.

You cannot delete a genuine review just because it is negative. A mix of reviews often looks more trustworthy than a perfect score.

## Where to see your reviews

Only reviews that break Google's policies can be removed, by reporting them. Genuine negative reviews stay, so reply calmly and fix the issue.
