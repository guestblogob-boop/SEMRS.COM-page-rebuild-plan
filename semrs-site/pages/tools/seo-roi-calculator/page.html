How the SEO ROI calculator works

The calculator follows the same chain every business goes through:

  1. Extra visits. Your current monthly organic visits multiplied by the increase you expect.
  2. Extra leads. Extra visits multiplied by your visitor-to-lead conversion rate (calls, forms, bookings or orders).
  3. Extra customers. Extra leads multiplied by your close rate.
  4. Extra revenue. Extra customers multiplied by your average customer value.
  5. Return. Revenue over 12 months compared with what you spend on SEO in the same period.

SEO results build gradually, so the calculator assumes your traffic grows evenly until it reaches your target in the month you choose, then stays there. The monthly table shows how the running total moves from investment to return.

Where to find your numbers

  • Organic visits: Google Search Console (Performance report, clicks) or Google Analytics 4 (traffic acquisition, organic search). Use a typical recent month.
  • Conversion rate: leads from organic visitors divided by organic visits. If you do not track this yet, count calls and form enquiries for a month.
  • Close rate: the share of leads that become paying customers. Your sales records or CRM will show this.
  • Customer value: the average revenue per customer. For repeat business, consider the value over a year or the customer's lifetime. For a stricter view, use profit instead of revenue.
  • Expected increase and timescale: be conservative. Look at your competitors, how much demand exists in your area and how much work your site needs.

Worked example

A roofing company in Denver gets about 2,000 organic visits a month. It assumes a 50% increase over nine months, a 2.5% enquiry rate, a 25% close rate, an average job value of $1,200 and an SEO budget of $1,000 a month. At full growth that is 1,000 extra visits, 25 extra enquiries, around 6 extra customers and about $7,500 of extra revenue a month. Because growth ramps up gradually, the calculator estimates roughly $60,000 of extra revenue against $12,000 of spend in the first year, a net of $48,000.

Then the owner tries a cautious scenario: a 25% increase over 12 months and a 1.5% enquiry rate. The return is much smaller, which shows how sensitive SEO value is to conversion. That is often the most useful insight: improving the website's ability to turn visitors into enquiries can matter as much as the extra traffic.

Reading the results honestly

This is a planning tool, not a promise. Real results depend on your market, competition, website, reviews, pricing and how quickly improvements are made. No agency can guarantee rankings or traffic, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Use the calculator to:

  • decide whether SEO is worth exploring at your budget,
  • compare cautious, likely and optimistic scenarios,
  • see which lever matters most: traffic, conversion, close rate or customer value,
  • set realistic expectations about timing.

Ways to improve your return

  • Improve conversion first. Clear calls to action, visible phone numbers, fast pages, prices or price guides, and trust signals such as genuine reviews.
  • Target searches with buying intent, such as "emergency electrician near me", not only informational topics.
  • Measure everything. Track calls and form submissions so you know your real conversion rate.
  • Follow up leads quickly. Response time strongly affects close rates for service businesses.
  • Give it time. SEO usually takes several months to show its full effect, and the gains tend to persist.

Common mistakes

  • Using a total visit number that includes paid and direct traffic.
  • Assuming very high growth in a short time.
  • Using revenue when margins are thin; check the profit view too.
  • Ignoring repeat customers and referrals, which can make the real value higher.
  • Comparing SEO costs for one month with results from the first month only.

Tips for local businesses

For local service businesses in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, organic search often brings the highest-intent customers: people searching for a service in their town right now. Your visit numbers may be modest compared with national websites, but conversion and close rates are often much higher. Include phone calls in your lead count, because many local customers call rather than fill in forms. If you serve several towns, consider running the calculator for each genuine service area separately.

Frequently asked questions

Is this calculator accurate?

It is only as accurate as the numbers you enter, and real growth is rarely perfectly smooth. Use it to compare scenarios and understand what drives value, not as a forecast or a guarantee.

How long does SEO take to pay off?

It varies widely. Many businesses see meaningful progress within four to twelve months, depending on competition, the state of the website and the work done.

What is a good conversion rate for a service website?

It varies by industry, offer and traffic source. Many service websites convert somewhere between 1% and 5% of visitors into enquiries. Measure your own rate and improve it.

Should I use revenue or profit for customer value?

Revenue shows the size of the opportunity; profit shows whether it is worth it. Running both gives a fuller picture.

Does the calculator include paid ads?

No. It estimates organic search value only. Paid ads stop when the budget stops, while SEO gains tend to build and last.
