Why meta tags still matter

Search results are a list of choices. For every search, a person scans a handful of titles and descriptions and picks one in seconds. Two pages ranking side by side can get very different numbers of clicks purely because one result is clearer about what it offers. Better snippets turn the rankings you already have into more visitors, without waiting to climb higher.

Google usually builds your result from your title tag and meta description, but it rewrites them when they are too long, stuffed with keywords or do not match the page. Writing accurate, specific tags of the right length is the best way to keep control of your result.

What to check on every page

  • A unique title that leads with the topic or service and fits within about 600 pixels on desktop.
  • A unique description of roughly 120–155 characters that explains the benefit and ends with a clear next step.
  • The main search phrase in the title, ideally near the start, written naturally.
  • The location in titles of local service pages, where it helps the searcher.
  • No duplicates across pages, which are common on sites built from templates.

Which meta and SERP tools to use

Use the SERP Snippet Preview when you want to see the finished result and test different wording visually, including how a date or a long URL affects the layout. Use the Meta Title & Description Checker when you want a checklist that confirms length, pixel width and keyword placement, and gives you ready-to-paste HTML and Open Graph tags.

A good workflow is to draft in the checker until everything passes, then confirm the look in the preview.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I update my titles and descriptions?

Review them when you change a page, when you launch new services, and when Search Console shows a page with many impressions but few clicks.

Do meta descriptions affect rankings?

Not directly. They affect how many people choose your result, which is why they are worth writing well.

Should every page have a meta description?

Every important page should. If it is missing, Google writes one from your page text, which may not say what you want.
