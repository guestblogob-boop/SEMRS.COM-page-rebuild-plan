Ecommerce SEO helps an online store's category and product pages show up when shoppers search for what it sells. Stores face problems other sites do not: hundreds of similar pages, thin product descriptions copied from suppliers, filters that create duplicate URLs and products that come and go. This guide covers the structure, page content and technical settings that help small stores rank.

## Key takeaways

- Plan a simple structure: home, categories, then products, with each category targeting a clear search.
- Write unique product and category text, and add product structured data so Google understands prices and stock.
- Control duplicate URLs from filters and sorting, and handle sold-out products carefully.

## Ecommerce SEO starts with the site structure

@img chart

Google's ecommerce guide includes advice on designing a URL structure for online stores. A clear hierarchy helps shoppers and search engines:

- **Home page** links to your main categories.
- **Category pages** target broad searches, such as "women's running shoes".
- **Subcategory pages** target narrower searches, such as "trail running shoes for women".
- **Product pages** target specific searches, such as a model name and size.

Keep URLs short and readable, such as /running-shoes/trail/. Every product should be reachable within a few clicks of the home page.

## Category pages: your biggest opportunity

Category pages often rank for the most valuable searches. Make each one useful:

- **A short intro** that explains the range and who it suits.
- **Helpful filters**, such as size, color and price.
- **Buying advice** below the products: how to choose, sizes and care.
- **Internal links** to related categories and guides.

## Product pages that sell and rank

| Element | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Title | Brand, product name and key detail, such as size or material |
| Description | Your own words: who it is for, how it is used, what makes it different |
| Photos | Several real photos, compressed, with descriptive alt text |
| Price and stock | Clear and accurate |
| Reviews | Real customer reviews on the page |
| Shipping and returns | Clear costs and times |

Avoid copying manufacturer descriptions word for word; hundreds of stores use the same text.

## Structured data and Merchant Center

Google's ecommerce guide recommends including structured data relevant to ecommerce and sharing your product data with Google. Product structured data tells Google the name, price, availability and reviews of a product. A Google Merchant Center feed can also make products eligible for free listings in Google Shopping. Most store platforms and SEO plugins can add product schema automatically; check it with Google's Rich Results Test.

## Technical issues stores must handle

- **Filter and sort URLs:** many combinations create near-duplicate pages. Use canonical tags and keep only useful filtered pages indexable.
- **Pagination:** make sure products on later pages can be found and crawled.
- **Sold-out products:** keep the page if the product will return; if it is gone for good, redirect to the closest product or category.
- **Speed:** large product images slow pages down. Our free [image compressor](/tools/image-compressor/) helps.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is ecommerce SEO?
Ecommerce SEO is improving an online store's structure, category pages, product pages and technical settings so they appear in search results for what shoppers look for.

? Should I write my own product descriptions?
Yes, where you can. Unique descriptions that explain use, fit and benefits help both shoppers and search, especially for your best-selling products.

? What should I do with out-of-stock products?
Keep the page if the product is coming back, with a clear stock message. If it is gone for good, redirect it to the closest alternative or its category.

? Do I need product structured data?
It is strongly recommended. Google's ecommerce guidance includes structured data, and it helps products show price, availability and reviews in search.
