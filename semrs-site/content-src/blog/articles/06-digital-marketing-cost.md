Your digital marketing cost can be almost nothing if you do it yourself, or many thousands a month if you hand several channels to an agency. What most small businesses really want to know is how to spend a sensible amount on the channels that will bring customers. This guide breaks down the cost of each channel, the ways agencies charge and a simple method for setting your budget.

## Key takeaways

- Each channel is priced differently: retainers for SEO, ad spend plus fees for paid ads, projects for websites.
- Fund one or two channels properly instead of spreading a small budget across six.
- Measure cost per lead and cost per customer, then move budget to what works.

## Digital marketing cost by channel at a glance

| Channel | How it is usually priced | What drives the cost |
|---|---|---|
| SEO | Monthly retainer | Competition, content volume, site condition |
| Google Ads and paid social | Ad spend plus a management fee | Industry, location, keywords, targeting |
| Website design | One-off project plus hosting and care | Pages, features, custom design, e-commerce |
| Content writing | Per article or monthly package | Length, research depth, subject expertise |
| Social media management | Monthly package | Platforms, posts per week, community replies |
| Email marketing | Software subscription plus setup or management | List size, automation, design |

## SEO

Most providers charge a monthly retainer. A widely cited Ahrefs survey found the most common band was $501 to $1,000 a month, with agencies averaging around $3,200 and freelancers around $1,350. Read the full [SEO cost guide](/how-much-does-seo-cost/) for what a fair retainer includes.

## Google Ads and paid social

Paid channels have two costs: what you pay the platform and what you pay someone to manage it. The 2025 WordStream by LocaliQ benchmarks put the average Google Ads cost per click near $5 across industries, with legal and financial services well above that. Our [Google Ads cost guide](/google-ads-cost/) shows how to work out a budget from your cost per customer.

## Website design

A small business website is usually a one-off project followed by monthly hosting and maintenance. Price depends on the number of pages, custom design, integrations such as booking or payments, and whether you need e-commerce. A fast, accessible site with clear calls to action pays for itself because every other channel sends visitors to it. See our [web design service](/services/web-design/) and [portfolio](/portfolio/) for examples.

## Content writing

Content is priced per piece or as a monthly package. Short, generic posts are cheap but rarely rank. In-depth articles researched around real keywords, with original examples and expert review, cost more and perform better. SEMRS [SEO content writing](/services/seo-content-writing/) is priced per article with a human review step.

## Social media management

Packages are priced by the number of platforms and posts per week, plus whether community management (replies and messages) is included. Ad spend for boosted posts is extra.

## Email marketing

Email software is a subscription that usually grows with your list size. Setup work, such as welcome sequences and templates, is a one-off cost; ongoing campaigns can be done in-house or managed monthly. Email often has the best return because you own the list.

## How agencies charge

- **Monthly retainer**: ongoing work for a fixed fee. Predictable for both sides.
- **Project fee**: a defined deliverable such as a website or an audit.
- **Hourly**: consulting or ad-hoc work.
- **Percentage of ad spend**: common for paid ads management; make sure the agency is not rewarded simply for spending more.

## How to set a digital marketing budget and control cost

1. **Know your numbers.** Before comparing any digital marketing cost, know your average sale value, profit margin and how many new customers you need each month.
2. **Pick one or two channels to start.** A local service business might start with a [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile-optimization/), a solid website and Google Ads. An online store might start with SEO, email and shopping ads.
3. **Fund each channel properly.** Spreading a small budget across six channels usually means none of them works.
4. **Measure cost per lead and cost per customer.** Then move budget to what works.
5. **Review every quarter.** Shift spend as SEO and email start bringing results that do not cost per click.

## Frequently asked questions

? How much should a small business spend on digital marketing?
There is no single rule. Many businesses set marketing as a share of revenue and then judge each channel by cost per customer. Start with an amount you can sustain for six months, because SEO and content take time to compound.

? What is the cheapest digital marketing channel?
Your Google Business Profile, email to existing customers and referrals cost the least in money, but they need consistent time. Paid ads cost the most per lead but work fastest.

? Is it cheaper to hire a freelancer or an agency?
Freelancers usually cost less for a single skill. Agencies cost more but cover several skills and provide continuity. Compare by what is delivered each month.

? Why do digital marketing quotes vary so much?
Scope, experience, industry competition and how much is included. Ask every provider for a written list of monthly deliverables and how results will be reported.
