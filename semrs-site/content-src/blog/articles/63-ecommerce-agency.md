An ecommerce agency helps online stores sell more: building or improving the store, bringing in traffic and turning visitors into buyers. Some agencies do everything; others focus on one part, such as store builds, search, paid ads or email. This guide explains what these agencies do, how they charge and the questions to ask before you hire one.

## Key takeaways

- Decide what you need first: a store build, more traffic, better conversion or all three.
- Make sure you own the store, ad accounts, analytics and data.
- Judge agencies by revenue and profit reports, not by traffic or likes alone.

## What an ecommerce agency does

| Service | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Store build or redesign | Platform setup, design, product pages, checkout |
| Ecommerce SEO | Category and product pages, structured data, technical fixes |
| Paid ads | Google Shopping, search and social ads |
| Email and retention | Welcome series, abandoned cart, repeat purchase emails |
| Conversion optimization | Testing pages, checkout and offers |
| Content | Buying guides, product descriptions, photos |

Our guide to [ecommerce SEO](/ecommerce-seo/) covers the search side in detail.

## How ecommerce agencies charge

- **Project fee** for a store build or redesign.
- **Monthly retainer** for ongoing SEO, content or management.
- **Percentage of ad spend** for paid ads.
- **Performance-based fees** tied to revenue, sometimes with a base fee.

Ask for fees in dollars at your current size, plus any setup fees and minimum terms.

## 8 questions to ask an ecommerce agency

@img chart

1. **Which platforms do you build on**, and why do you recommend one for us?
2. **Who owns the store, domain, ad accounts and analytics?** It should be you.
3. **What will you do in the first 90 days?**
4. **How do you report results?** Look for revenue, profit and cost per sale.
5. **Can we see stores you have worked on** and talk to a client?
6. **Who will work on our account** day to day?
7. **What does it cost each month**, and what is the minimum term?
8. **How do we leave**, and what do we keep if we do?

## Red flags

- Guaranteed rankings or sales numbers.
- The agency owns your store or ad accounts.
- Long contracts with exit fees before results are proven.
- Reports full of traffic and impressions but no revenue.
- No clear plan for your margins and product costs.

## In-house, freelancer or agency?

| Option | Good when |
|---|---|
| In-house | You have steady work for a full-time person |
| Freelancer | You need one skill, such as store design or email |
| Agency | You need several skills working together |

Many stores mix them: an agency for ads and SEO, a freelancer for photos, and in-house staff for customer service.

## Frequently asked questions

? What does an ecommerce agency do?
It helps online stores grow by building or improving the store and running SEO, ads, email and conversion work, depending on the services you choose.

? How much do these agencies cost?
It depends on the services and pricing model: project fees for builds, monthly retainers for ongoing work, or a share of ad spend. Ask for the total in dollars at your size.

? Should I hire an agency or a freelancer?
A freelancer suits one clear skill; an agency suits stores that need several skills working together. Either way, keep accounts in your name.

? What should an agency report each month?
Revenue, orders, average order value, cost per sale and the actions taken and planned, not just traffic.
