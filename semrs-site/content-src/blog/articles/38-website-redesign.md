A website redesign can bring more leads, a faster site and a stronger brand, but a careless one can wipe out years of search traffic overnight. The difference is planning: knowing which pages bring visitors, keeping their addresses or redirecting them, and testing before launch. This guide walks through the process in nine steps.

## Key takeaways

- Start with goals and data: which pages bring traffic and leads today.
- Map every old URL to a new one and set permanent (301) redirects; Google advises keeping them for at least a year.
- Test speed, mobile layout, forms and tracking on a staging copy before you launch.

## Signs you need a website redesign

- The site is slow or hard to use on phones.
- Visitors arrive but few call, book or buy.
- Your services, prices or brand have changed.
- The site is hard to update without a developer.
- It fails basic accessibility checks or looks out of date next to competitors.

If only one or two of these apply, targeted fixes may be cheaper than a full redesign.

## The website redesign process in 9 steps

@img chart

### 1. Set goals you can measure

"More leads from the website" becomes "20 quote requests a month". Clear goals guide every design choice.

### 2. Audit your current site

Export your pages and their traffic from Google Search Console and analytics. Note which pages bring visitors, rank for important searches or earn links. These pages must survive the redesign.

### 3. Decide what stays, changes or goes

| Page status | Action |
|---|---|
| Brings traffic or leads | Keep the address; improve the content |
| Useful but weak | Rewrite or merge into a stronger page |
| Outdated with no traffic | Remove and redirect to the closest page |
| Missing | Add pages for services people search for |

### 4. Plan the structure and content

Sketch the menu and page list. Write or update content before design starts, so the design fits real text.

### 5. Map old URLs to new URLs

Google's guide to moving a site recommends a mapping of every old URL to its new URL. Keep addresses the same where you can. Where they change, the map becomes your redirect list.

### 6. Design and build on a staging site

Build on a private copy, not the live site. Check that it works on phones, loads fast and is easy to read. Our [ADA website compliance guide](/ada-website-compliance/) covers accessibility basics.

### 7. Set up 301 redirects

Use server-side permanent redirects (301 or 308) from each old URL to its new match. Google advises keeping redirects for as long as possible, generally at least one year.

### 8. Test before launch

- Every form sends and reaches the right inbox.
- Analytics and conversion tracking work.
- Old URLs redirect to the right new pages.
- Titles, meta descriptions and internal links are in place.
- The site passes speed checks on mobile.

### 9. Launch and monitor

Submit your new sitemap in Search Console. Watch traffic, rankings and errors for the next few weeks, and fix broken links quickly.

## How much does a website redesign cost?

A redesign usually costs about the same as a new site with the same number of pages, because most of the work is content and design. Our guide to [website design cost](/website-design-cost/) compares options and lists the running costs to budget for.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Changing URLs without redirects**, the most common cause of lost traffic.
- **Deleting pages that ranked** because they did not fit the new design.
- **Launching without tracking**, so you cannot measure results.
- **Choosing looks over speed**, with heavy sliders and large images.
- **Redesigning the platform and content at once** without a plan for either.

## Should you change platforms during a redesign?

Sometimes it makes sense, for example moving from a hosted builder to WordPress for more control. It adds risk, so plan the URL map and redirects with extra care. Our guide to [website builders for small business](/website-builders-for-small-business/) compares the options.

## Frequently asked questions

? How long does a website redesign take?
Small business redesigns often take a few weeks to a few months, depending on the number of pages, who writes the content and how quickly decisions are made.

? Will a redesign hurt my SEO?
It should not if you keep important URLs or redirect them, keep good content and test before launch. Most traffic losses come from missing redirects or deleted pages.

? How long should I keep 301 redirects after a redesign?
Google advises keeping them for as long as possible, generally at least one year.

? How often should a business redesign its website?
There is no fixed rule. Redesign when the site no longer meets your goals, works poorly on phones or no longer reflects your business. Many sites only need regular updates in between.
