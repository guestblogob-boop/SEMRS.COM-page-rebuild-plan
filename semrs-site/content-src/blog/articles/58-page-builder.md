A page builder is a WordPress plugin that lets you design pages by dragging and dropping elements, with more layout options than a basic editor. Since 2018, WordPress has had its own visual editor built in: the block editor. So do you still need one? This guide compares the two on speed, flexibility, cost and lock-in, and helps you choose.

## Key takeaways

- The block editor is built into WordPress, free, and creates lighter pages; it covers most small business needs.
- Page builders offer more visual control out of the box, but can add weight and make switching harder.
- Choose one approach for the whole site and keep it consistent.

## What the block editor is

WordPress.org describes the block editor as the way to publish content in WordPress; it became the default in WordPress 5.0 in December 2018. Every paragraph, image, heading, button and gallery is a block. Blocks can be grouped into columns and sections, saved as reusable patterns and, with block themes, used to edit headers, footers and templates in the Site Editor.

## What a builder plugin adds

A builder replaces or sits beside the editor with its own drag-and-drop canvas. Typical extras:

- **Detailed design controls** for spacing, effects and animations.
- **Large template libraries** for whole pages.
- **Theme building** for headers, footers and archive pages.
- **Popups and forms**, often in paid versions.

## When the block editor is the better choice

- You want a fast site that scores well on Core Web Vitals.
- Your pages follow a few simple layouts: services, about, blog posts.
- You want to avoid yearly license fees.
- You want content that will keep working if you change themes.

## When a builder plugin makes sense

- You need complex, highly visual landing pages often and want to build them yourself.
- Your team already knows a specific builder well.
- Your theme was built around a builder and rebuilding is not worth it yet.

If you use one, keep pages light: avoid heavy sliders and animations, and test speed after each new page.

## Switching later

Moving from a builder plugin to blocks usually means rebuilding page layouts, while the text and images can be kept. Plan it like a redesign; our guide to [website redesign](/website-redesign/) covers URL mapping and testing.

## Tips for building pages with blocks

1. **Start from patterns** for common sections such as hero, features and FAQs.
2. **Use a theme with good spacing and typography** so pages look polished without custom code.
3. **Save your own patterns** for sections you reuse.
4. **Keep one heading per section** and short paragraphs.
5. **Check every page on a phone** before publishing.

## Frequently asked questions

? What is a page builder in WordPress?
It is a plugin that lets you design pages with a drag-and-drop canvas and extra design controls, beyond the built-in block editor.

? Is the block editor good enough for a business site?
For most small business sites, yes. It handles service pages, landing pages and blogs, and keeps pages light and fast.

? Do page builders slow down WordPress?
They can, because many add their own CSS and scripts. Test speed with PageSpeed Insights after building pages.

? Can I switch to the block editor later?
Yes, but layouts usually need rebuilding. Text and images can be reused.
