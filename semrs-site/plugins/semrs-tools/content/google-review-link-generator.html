Why a direct review link matters

Most happy customers are willing to leave a review, but very few will search for your business, find your profile, scroll to the reviews section and click the right button. Every extra step loses people. A direct review link removes those steps: one tap opens the star rating and comment box for your business, on desktop or mobile.

Reviews matter for local businesses for two reasons. They influence customers, who read them before calling or visiting. And Google lists review quantity and quality among the signals that help a business stand out in local results. A steady flow of genuine reviews, with thoughtful replies from you, is one of the most effective things a local business can do online.

How to find your Google Place ID

Your Place ID is a unique code Google gives every business listing. It usually starts with "ChIJ" and has no spaces.

  1. Open Google's free Place ID Finder using the link in the tool.
  2. Type your business name in the search box on the map and choose your listing.
  3. Copy the Place ID shown in the info window.
  4. Paste it into the tool along with your business name.

If your business does not appear, check that your Google Business Profile is verified and visible on Google Maps.

When and how to ask for reviews

  • Ask soon after a good experience, when the job is finished or the order has arrived. The memory is fresh.
  • Ask every customer, not just the ones you think are happy.
  • Keep it short and personal. Use the customer's name and mention what you did for them.
  • Make it one tap. Send the direct link, not instructions.
  • Reply to every review, positive or negative, politely and specifically. Future customers read your replies.
  • Spread requests over time. A steady flow looks natural; a sudden burst can be filtered.

Google's review rules you must follow

Google's policies are strict, and breaking them can get reviews removed or a profile restricted:

  • No incentives. Never offer discounts, gifts, entries into prize draws or payment for reviews.
  • No review gating. Do not ask only customers you expect to be positive, or filter people through a "were you happy?" step that sends unhappy customers elsewhere.
  • No fake reviews. Do not write reviews yourself, ask staff to review the business or buy reviews.
  • No review stations or pressure. Let customers review in their own time on their own devices.

Tips for local businesses in the US, UK, Canada and Australia

Customers increasingly compare local businesses on Google before they call, whether they are looking for a plumber in Phoenix, a hairdresser in Bristol, a dentist in Ottawa or a mechanic in Perth. Reviews that mention the service and the area naturally ("fixed our boiler in Clifton the same day") help other customers decide, but never script those words for customers. Ask, make it easy, and let customers describe the experience in their own words.

Frequently asked questions

Is the review link free and permanent?

Yes. It is a standard Google URL built from your Place ID. It keeps working as long as your Business Profile exists and the Place ID does not change.

Can I offer a discount for leaving a review?

No. Google prohibits incentives for reviews, including discounts, gifts and prize draws. Offering them can lead to reviews being removed.

Can I ask only my happiest customers?

No. Selectively asking satisfied customers, known as review gating, is against Google's policies. Ask everyone in the same way.

How should I respond to a negative review?

Reply calmly and quickly, thank them, acknowledge the issue without arguing, and offer to resolve it offline. A good reply often reassures future customers more than a five-star review.

My Place ID is not accepted. What is wrong?

Place IDs are long codes, usually starting with "ChIJ", with no spaces. Copy it again from the Place ID Finder, making sure you have not included extra text.
