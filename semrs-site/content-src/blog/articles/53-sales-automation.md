Sales automation takes repetitive tasks off your team's plate: logging new leads, sending the first reply, reminding someone to follow up and moving deals to the next stage. For a small team, it means fewer forgotten leads and more time for real conversations. This guide shows what to automate first, six workflows you can copy and the rules for automated email and texts.

## Key takeaways

- Automate the boring, repeatable steps; keep real conversations human.
- Start with instant lead replies, follow-up reminders and pipeline updates.
- Automated emails must follow CAN-SPAM, and marketing texts need prior written consent.

[glance: Sales automation at a glance | Reply instantly | Never miss a follow-up | Keep one record | Stay human]

## What to automate (and what not to)

| Automate | Keep human |
|---|---|
| Logging new leads in your CRM | The first real conversation |
| An instant "we got your request" reply | Quotes and proposals for complex jobs |
| Follow-up reminders and tasks | Handling objections |
| Moving deals between stages | Negotiating price |
| Appointment reminders | Saying thank you after a big sale |

## 6 sales automation workflows to copy

@img chart

1. **New lead capture:** form or call → contact created in the CRM → owner assigned.
2. **Instant reply:** new lead → email or text (with consent) confirming you received the request and when you will call.
3. **Speed-to-lead alert:** no call logged within one hour → reminder to the owner and their manager.
4. **Quote follow-up:** quote sent → reminder after three days if there is no reply → second reminder after a week.
5. **Appointment reminders:** booking confirmed → reminder the day before.
6. **Won or lost:** deal closed → review request for won deals, short feedback question for lost ones.

## Rules for automated messages

**Email.** The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide applies to commercial email: accurate headers, honest subject lines, a postal address and an opt-out honored within 10 business days. One-to-one transactional replies, such as confirming a request, have fewer rules, but keep them honest.

**Texts and calls.** Automated marketing texts and calls generally need the person's prior express written consent under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. Record when and how each person agreed.

## Common mistakes

- **Automating a broken process.** Fix the steps first, then automate them.
- **Too many automated messages**, which feel like spam and raise unsubscribes.
- **No owner for each lead**, so reminders go to nobody.
- **Not testing workflows** with a real form before going live.
- **Forgetting to update templates** when prices or services change.

## Measuring results

Track:

- **Speed to first contact.**
- **Lead-to-appointment rate.**
- **Quote-to-win rate.**
- **Leads with no follow-up** (this should fall to zero).

## Frequently asked questions

? What is sales automation?
Sales automation uses software to handle repetitive sales tasks, such as logging leads, sending first replies, setting reminders and updating deal stages.

? What should a small team automate first?
Start with lead capture into a CRM, an instant reply to new enquiries and follow-up reminders. These stop leads from slipping through.

? Will automation make my sales feel impersonal?
Not if you automate tasks, not conversations. Use automation to make sure a person calls quickly and follows up on time.

? Do I need consent to send automated texts to leads?
For marketing texts, you generally need prior express written consent. Keep records and honor opt-outs.
